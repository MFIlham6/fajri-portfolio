**A Revenue Target Was About to Be Built on 24.6% Growth, Yet Early-Year Volume Had Already Stopped Rising**  
Revenue forecast, mid-sized company, Spreadsheet, 482 clean financial records from 500 raw rows, 27 months  
Mochamad Fajri Ilham Nurdin, completed October 2026, mfajriilhamn@gmail.com  
*Data note: the figures below come from a simulated dataset that mimics the financial records of a mid-sized company. It is not real company data, and it is not presented as work experience at any company.*  
**Summary.** A mid-sized company was about to set its annual revenue target from the previous year's 24.6% growth. That figure is correct, but breaking it down shows all of the increase came from a higher number of transactions, and in the first quarter of the current year that number had stopped rising. This work produces a forecast as a range, one budget figure with its reasoning, and a list of indicators that can be tracked monthly.  
**Why this report exists.** The target figure decides more than the budget document: it becomes the basis for each team's target, hiring plans, and how much cost may be planned. The head of finance asked for one number for the budget meeting. Too high, and the whole organization chases volume it doesn't have the capacity for; too low, and resources aren't prepared. That decision will be made with or without an analysis.  
**Data.** 27 months of financial records, five hundred rows, with date, revenue, cost, profit, and five categories. 482 rows remain after cleaning. There is no customer data, unit count, or event marker such as campaigns or price changes, and cost is recorded as a single combined figure. The latest period contains only three months.  
**What I did.** I broke year-on-year growth into two components: the number of transactions and the average value per transaction. I tested the pattern against all 27 months with correlation, then repeated it by category to check whether the mechanism holds everywhere. The forecast locks the component that proved stable and varies the one that moves. Rejected approach: treating the last three months as an annual data point, because as a total it appears to fall 74.3% when the period is only a quarter of a year. Also rejected: deciding cleaning wasn't needed because its effect on the total is only 1.4%. In the monthly breakdown, which is the basis of the forecast, eleven of twelve months shift, by up to 18.7%.

**Findings.**

> * Revenue rose 24.6%, transaction count rose 26.9%, and average value per transaction fell 1.8%. All of the growth came from volume, and this pattern holds in all five categories.  
> * Across 27 months, monthly revenue correlates 0.967 with transaction count, while value per transaction correlates −0.194 with time, barely moving around Rp 32.3 million.  
> * The latest period gives three conclusions depending on how it is compared: down 74.3%, up 2.9%, or down 12.8%. Only the third compares like with like, and there the transaction count fell 10.4%. Three months are too few to confirm a direction, but enough not to assume last year's pace will continue.  
> * Cleaning 18 rows shifts the total by only 1.4%, but it shifts eleven of the twelve monthly figures in the second year, the largest by 18.7%, so the shape of the curve used for forecasting changes even though the total is almost the same.

**What changed in the decision.** Before the analysis, the target would have been set by continuing the 24.6% growth. Afterwards, it is built by multiplying two components: value per transaction, locked because it is stable, and transaction count, varied from flat to repeating last year's pace. The result is a range of Rp 7.6 to 9.7 billion with a midpoint of Rp 8.7 billion. The proposed budget figure is Rp 7.6 billion, because the first-quarter transaction pace matches the flat scenario, and the gap to the upper bound is a management decision about adding capacity that hasn't been made yet. The indicator to track also changes, from revenue to transactions per month.  
**Limits.** The data has no customers or unit counts, so the cause of the volume increase can't be confirmed: it could be more customers, existing customers ordering more often, or a change in how records are kept. A 27-month span is too short to claim a seasonal pattern, so the first-quarter drop can't be separated into season versus slowdown. The forecast is about a period that hasn't happened yet, so what can be judged is its assumptions, not the accuracy of its numbers.
